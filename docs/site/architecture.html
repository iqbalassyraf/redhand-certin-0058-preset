Architecture

The shield sits on the tool-execution boundary. The agent, and anything that has manipulated the agent, can only reach a tool through it. No model is in the decision path: every decision is a pure function of the signed token, the declarative policy, the clock and sliding-window state.

The four stages

  1. Quarantine. A run, or every run of a token subject, that tripped a breaker is refused before anything else is evaluated. This also stops an attacker from flooding the log.
  2. Identity. The Ed25519 run token must verify, belong to this run, name the audience, be unrevoked and unexpired. Its environment must equal the call's target_env, and the tool must be in the signed allowed_tools. This is the staging-to-prod pivot check.
  3. Off-hours. On weekends, holidays and outside business hours the policy is tightened as a pure function of policy and clock: limits halve and approval becomes mandatory.
  4. Gates and detectors. Stateless approval gates, then the sliding-window detectors under a short lock. Detector state lives behind the WindowStore protocol.

Enforce mode stops the call with a typed exception that carries the finding. Observe-only mode lets it through but records would_have_blocked, so a policy can be rolled out safely.

Fail closed

A missing or malformed context, an unverifiable token, a full detector table or a store error all produce a block, never a silent allow. The threat model lists what is defended and, just as important, what is not.
